Overview

The problem

Getting around Jonesboro without a car is hard. Public transport is limited, and a quick trip to Walmart or Kroger can take a lot of planning.

Plenty of people with cars have free time. JoRide connects the two with the simplest possible loop: request a ride, and whoever is free takes it. No prices, no fares, just neighbours helping out.

Approach

How it works

  1. Request

    A rider picks a destination (with quick picks like Walmart and Kroger), a pickup point and either Now or a scheduled time.

  2. Accept

    Any driver who is online sees the request and taps "I can take you". Only one driver can win, because accepting is a single conditional database update.

  3. Ride

    Rider and driver get chat, quick replies and each other's phone number, plus a tracker that moves from requested to driver found, picked up and arrived.

  4. Finish safely

    The rider confirms "I arrived safely" and rates the driver. If a driver can't make it, the request goes straight back to open, and either side can report a problem.

Decisions

Key decisions

Two modes, one account

Sign-in asks whether you're riding or driving. Passenger mode is a light green app, and driver mode is a dark, amber interface with an online switch and a live request feed.

Phone-first everywhere

Key actions are pinned to the bottom of the screen within thumb reach, and the app can be installed to the home screen like a native app.

Kept deliberately free

Earlier ideas such as splitting gas costs, a student-only gate and drivers posting seat offers were dropped to keep the loop as simple as asking a friend.

Simple, local data

SQLite stores users, sessions, rides, messages, ratings and reports, which is fast to build on before moving to a hosted database.

Next

What's next

Put it online with a hosted database, add phone or email verification for safety, and notify riders the moment a driver accepts.
