Overview

The problem

In the US, becoming a licensed civil engineer means passing the FE exam, gaining experience and then passing the PE. I'm working toward that path, and the hard part isn't motivation. It's structure: what to study, in what order, and how to know you're actually ready.

Good free material exists, but it's scattered across MIT OpenCourseWare, NPTEL, Khan Academy and government sites, and most structured FE prep is paid. I wanted one place that turns all of that into a plan you can follow and check off.

Approach

What I built

  1. A roadmap stored as data

    9 phases, 36 modules and 94 tasks, each with key concepts and hand-picked resources tagged free, student or paid. Every link was checked live, and task IDs stay stable so saved progress never breaks when content changes.

  2. Practice that mirrors the exam

    145 FE-style questions across 12 topics with worked solutions, per-topic accuracy, a review-missed mode and an FE-pace timer. Each phase ends in a test (pass mark 70%), and a 40-question final exam (75%) awards a printable certificate.

  3. Calculators for checking hand work

    Beam analysis with shear, moment and deflection diagrams, section properties, Manning's equation, a concrete estimator, engineering economics and a unit converter, all verified against hand calculations.

  4. Habits, not just content

    A study log with a weekly goal, a 16-week heatmap and hours by subject, so progress is visible week to week.

Decisions

Key decisions

Guest-first, accounts optional

Everything works without signing up, with progress kept in the browser. An optional free account syncs to Postgres and merges guest progress on first login, so nobody loses work.

Security done properly

Passwords are bcrypt-hashed, sessions are random tokens in httpOnly cookies with only their SHA-256 stored, failed logins are throttled and password-reset links are single-use and expire in an hour.

Honest about cost

A dedicated page explains what's genuinely free and what isn't, for example the FE exam fee itself, so learners can plan a zero-cost route.

Ships on every push

The site deploys automatically from GitHub to Vercel, so content fixes go live in minutes.
