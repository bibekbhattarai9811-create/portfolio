Overview

The problem

Making one Why It Broke episode took hours: writing the script in a chat window, recording voice files, generating clips, collecting archival footage, then placing everything by hand in a video editor.

Most of that time went into mechanical work: lining shots up with the narration, adding zooms and captions, trimming and exporting. That's exactly what software is good at.

Approach

The pipeline

  1. Plan

    Type an idea. Claude writes the script, shot list, image and video prompts, the cue word each shot should land on, and the title, description, tags and thumbnail idea.

  2. Voice

    Each narration segment is generated with a neural voice, along with word-level timings, or I upload my own recording.

  3. Quick draft

    A watchable draft renders immediately, with labelled placeholder cards for shots that aren't made yet, so the pacing can be judged early.

  4. Fill in visuals

    Drop in generated clips and archival footage. A per-shot "skip first seconds" setting trims footage to the right moment.

  5. Auto-edit and export

    Every shot is placed on its cue word, with slow zooms, slow motion, captions, music at 12% and a 1080p export, plus a publish kit with titles and tags ready to copy.

Decisions

Key decisions

A custom timeline, about 7× faster

MoviePy's built-in compositing was too slow for long videos, so the editor renders through a single custom clip that decides frame by frame which shot is visible.

Keep humans where they add value

AI video generation stays a copy-paste step in the Gemini app. It isn't available through a free API, and choosing the right clip is still a creative call.

Runs locally, works anywhere

Rendering happens on my PC. An optional password-protected tunnel lets me start and check renders from my phone.

Editable at every step

Any narration line can be edited, and its voice is regenerated automatically on the next render.

Results

Outcome

What used to take hours of manual editing is now mostly waiting for a render. I've used it to plan and render episodes on the Silver Bridge and Hyatt Regency walkway collapses.
